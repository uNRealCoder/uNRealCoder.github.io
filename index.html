About

The part after deployment

Plenty of machine learning works in a notebook and falls over the first time real traffic hits it. My work is the part that has to survive that: the infrastructure that turns live data into predictions, fast, and the same way every time.

At Mastercard I build inference infrastructure for fraud detection. The core of it is a graph feature library that data science teams use to turn raw transaction data into GNN embeddings, on graphs with billions of edges. Those embeddings feed fraud models that run in production and have stopped more than $100M in fraudulent transactions. I moved the whole graph pipeline off CPUs onto GPUs with NVIDIA RAPIDS and cuGraph, which made it roughly 50× faster — jobs that used to run for hours now finish in minutes.

The other half is language models. I deployed production LLMs with vLLM and large embedding models with ONNX behind agentic workflows that fraud investigators use to pull the case knowledge they need in minutes instead of days. I also work closely with several of those data science teams, turning promising prototypes into systems that stay up and can be watched.

Before Mastercard I did research at the University of Georgia, where I earned an M.S. in Artificial Intelligence. My thesis stacked several models together to predict stock prices; alongside it I used NLP to measure how social-media bot activity moves stock and crypto returns. Earlier I was a .NET engineer at CenturyLink, building web applications for law enforcement.

On weekends I build RAG systems and agents with LangGraph, CrewAI and AutoGen, mostly to find out where they break.
